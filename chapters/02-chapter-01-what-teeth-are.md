# Chapter 1: What Teeth Actually Are

A tooth is not a white pebble. It is a layered organ built to cut, crush, sense, and survive in a wet microbial environment.

Enamel is the hard outer shell of the crown. It is mostly mineral, especially hydroxyapatite crystals, and it has no living cells. That is why enamel can remineralize at the surface but cannot regrow once a chunk is drilled away or broken. Dentin sits underneath. It is less mineralized, more sensitive, and threaded with tiny tubules that can transmit cold, sweetness, and touch toward the pulp. The pulp is the living core, with nerves and blood vessels.

Below the gumline, cementum covers the root, the periodontal ligament suspends the tooth, and jaw bone holds the whole system in place. Gums are not decorative trim. They are a seal and an immune border. When that border stays inflamed, the attachment around the tooth can break down.

The sticky fact: the part you see is not the whole tooth. A tooth can look acceptable in the mirror while decay is forming between teeth, bone is being lost around roots, or a crack is traveling under a filling. That is why home care and professional evaluation have different jobs.
