# Author Voice Reference

The samples below are evidence of the author's natural voice.

Reproduce the underlying qualities, sentence movement, clarity, discernment, and degree of directness. Do not copy distinctive phrases or personal stories unless they belong in the manuscript.

When the samples conflict with generic AI writing conventions, follow the samples.

The finished book should sound like a polished, organized version of this author, not like institutional health content and not like an imitation of a textbook.

## Voice Notes

The voice is personal without becoming confessional, practical without becoming flat, and skeptical without becoming conspiratorial. It explains mechanisms because the reader deserves to know why. It is comfortable saying that a routine is not the cheap version, that adult habits are real, and that lecturing people is usually less effective than giving them usable mitigation steps.

Use humor sparingly. The sharpness should help the reader feel awake, not shamed.

## Sample: Foreword Seed

When I was in college, a magazine told me to brush after coffee to prevent stains. I ended up with tooth sensitivity and weakened enamel.

A few years ago, I went through my insurance's approved-dentist list just to end up at a place that wanted to do silver instead of white fillings, then would only take copay in cash.

Later, I wanted a perfect smile and found a clinic in Turkey through an online ad. They said yes to veneers. A few messages later, they were pushing crowns instead. Another clinic abroad told me I would not need retainers after veneers. Turns out, that is how veneers crack.

As kids, we are told to brush and floss. Then we graduate into a world full of delicious food and drinks with little knowledge of how to take care of one of the most important factors in health, intimacy, beauty, and longevity: our teeth.

Good smile care means we get to be beautiful longer, spend less time and money in dental chairs, and skip most of the pain that comes from finding out too late. If you have wondered if dental care is going to hurt, or had no idea where to even start finding a dentist you could trust while yearning for some gorgeous pearly whites, this book is for you.

I realized I knew nothing about adult dental care but wanted perfect teeth, and after four cavities I went on a dental journey. I am turning it into a book because I want to keep it to read it as a reminder. I also need a cheat sheet for my bathroom mirror in the form of an attractive poster. I think this is something others could benefit from too.

## Sample: Direct Practical Copy

Never brush immediately after food, coffee, tea, soda, or lemon water.

Acid softens enamel. Brushing right after scrubs the softened layer instead of protecting it. Wait, swish instead, brush later.

Best case is waiting 30 to 60 minutes. If that is not happening because you are heading into a meeting, about to kiss someone, or simply need your mouth to feel normal, swish first, wait at least a few minutes, use nHA toothpaste, use light pressure, and keep it short.

This is harm reduction, not a replacement for the Golden Rule.

## Sample: Adult Habit Framing

We are helping adults deal with habits, not policing behavior. Explain what each habit does, how to mitigate the damage, and what quitting changes. Do not jump straight to "vaping is not safer" as if no one has ever heard a lecture before. No one believes you when the tone gets too scoldy, and lecturing adults is not cute. Ironically, meeting people where they are tends to get people to change more anyway.
