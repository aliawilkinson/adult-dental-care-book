# Overnight Codex Cloud Task

Paste this task into a Codex cloud task connected to this repository.

Build a complete, researched first-draft manuscript for **The Adult Teeth Guide: How to Keep Your Teeth Healthy for Life** from the materials in this repository.

You are acting as:

- principal writer
- international dental-health researcher
- evidence reviewer
- developmental editor
- fact-checker
- continuity editor

Read every repository instruction and source file before drafting, including:

- `AGENTS.md`
- `outline.md`
- `author-voice.md`
- `editorial-rules.md`
- `claims-policy.md`
- `international-research-standard.md`
- all files under `sources/`
- all existing files under `research/`, `cheat-sheets/`, `chapters/`, and `reviews/`

## Central Research Requirement

This is an internationally researched dental-health book.

Do not treat the United States as the default authority and then append minor international examples.

Use current international evidence, international outcome data, major systematic reviews, WHO resources, Global Burden of Disease research, Cochrane reviews, national clinical guidance, and primary research.

Identify countries with strong oral-health outcomes separately by metric. Do not rely on generic "best dental health country" rankings.

Investigate countries including Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Sweden, Finland, Denmark, Norway, Iceland, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and the United States, plus any other country with especially relevant evidence or outcomes.

Compare:

- untreated decay
- childhood caries
- adult caries
- periodontal disease
- tooth retention
- complete tooth loss
- access
- affordability
- inequality
- prevention systems
- clinical intervention patterns

Do not infer that a national practice caused a national outcome merely because they occur together.

Do not assume that American approval establishes effectiveness.

Do not assume that international popularity establishes effectiveness.

Follow the evidence.

## Phase 1: Repository and Outline Analysis

## Phase 2: International Outcomes Research

Before drafting recommendations, create or update `research/international-outcomes-comparison.md`.

Use the latest reasonably comparable sources available.

For every outcome comparison:

- identify the source
- identify the data year
- identify the age group
- identify the metric
- identify whether the data are observed, modeled, or self-reported
- explain comparability limitations

Identify multiple high-performing countries by metric instead of declaring a single universal winner.

Then investigate which preventive behaviors, products, policies, clinical practices, and access structures appear consistently across high-performing systems.

Clearly distinguish observed association, plausible contribution, supported causal evidence, and speculation.

## Phase 3: International Guideline Matrix

Create or update `research/international-guideline-matrix.md`.

Compare guidance from multiple countries for every major topic covered in the outline.

Include the issuing organization, publication date, jurisdiction, exact recommendation, evidence grade when available, and major differences from other guidance.

Do not flatten disagreements into false consensus.

When guidance conflicts, evaluate the underlying evidence and explain the conflict.

## Phase 4: Claim Map and Evidence Ledger

Create or update:

- `research/claim-map.md`
- `research/evidence-ledger.md`
- `research/unresolved-evidence.md`

Map every consequential medical or scientific claim to appropriate evidence.

The first preference is high-quality evidence, not American familiarity.

Verify every citation against the original source.

Never fabricate citations, quotations, statistics, DOIs, guidelines, or national recommendations.

Mark claims that still require verification.

## Phase 5: Nano-Hydroxyapatite Review

Create or update `research/nano-hydroxyapatite-review.md`.

Conduct a serious international review including Japanese, European, and other relevant research.

Separate evidence concerning:

- remineralization
- caries prevention
- noninferiority to fluoride
- superiority to fluoride
- dentin hypersensitivity
- white-spot lesions
- safety
- particle characteristics
- concentration
- formulation
- clinical versus laboratory outcomes

Do not dismiss nano-hydroxyapatite because it is less established in American dental guidance.

Do not overstate it merely because it is used internationally.

Represent the strongest current evidence accurately.

Use the completed review when drafting the relevant chapters.

## Phase 7: Review Passes

Perform separate reviews for dental and medical accuracy, international bias, author voice, developmental continuity, and adversarial weakness.

### Dental and Medical Accuracy

Identify false, overstated, unsafe, incomplete, or outdated claims.

### International Bias

Create or update `reviews/international-bias-audit.md`.

Check whether the manuscript:

- defaults unnecessarily to American guidance
- ignores strong non-American research
- treats American regulation as scientific truth
- romanticizes international practices without evidence
- makes invalid country comparisons
- confuses health-system policy with individual clinical evidence
- overlooks disagreement between high-performing countries

Revise the manuscript where needed.

### Author Voice

Remove generic AI prose, canned health-writing language, patronizing reassurance, repetitive introductions, repetitive conclusions, needless disclaimers, excessive bullets, institutional vagueness, appeals to authority without reasoning, and em dashes.

Match the authorial qualities demonstrated in `author-voice.md`.

### Developmental Continuity

Check chapter order, duplication, terminology, transitions, and contradictions.

### Adversarial Review

Challenge the strongest and most controversial claims as an evidence-literate dentist, researcher, and skeptical reader would.

Do not protect claims merely because they align with the author's prior view.

Do not replace well-supported claims merely because they differ from conventional American practice.

Record findings in the appropriate review files and revise the chapter files where warranted.
