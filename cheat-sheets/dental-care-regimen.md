# The Dental Care Regimen

The whole book, boiled down to one poster. Print it, tape it to your bathroom mirror, and let your teeth eavesdrop on their own instructions.

---

## Your Kit

- Electric toothbrush + soft heads
- Copper tongue scraper
- 10% nHA toothpaste
- nHA mouthwash tablets
- Remineralizing mouthwash or drops
- Glide floss
- Water flosser
- Hydrogen peroxide, for cleaning tools only, never your mouth
- Mouth guard, if you grind

---

## The Four Things Trying to Ruin Your Smile

Enamel is the hardest tissue your body makes, harder than bone, and it still loses fights every day. Here is who it's fighting.

| Enemy | What it actually does | Why you should care |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plaque** | A film of bacteria that rebuilds on your teeth within hours of cleaning, no matter how good your last brush was | Left alone, it turns into tartar, which only a dental tool can remove, and it inflames your gums |
| **Acid** | Softens the mineral surface of enamel on contact, from food, drinks, or bacterial byproducts of sugar | Enamel doesn't grow back. Once the surface is gone, it's gone for good |
| **Force** | Grinding, clenching, aggressive brushing, ice chewing, using teeth as tools | Wears enamel down mechanically, cracks it, or recedes gums, all separate from the acid problem |
| **Delay** | Waiting on a small problem because it doesn't hurt yet | Small, cheap problems become large, expensive ones. Pain is a late symptom, not an early warning |

Your entire routine exists to interrupt these four things. That's it. Everything below is just tactics.

## The Routine, By Clock

| When | Do this | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Every morning** | Scrape tongue, drink water, brush gently with nHA toothpaste, then give it 20 to 30 minutes before eating or drinking | Lets the toothpaste sit on enamel and work instead of getting rinsed down your throat with your coffee |
| **After every meal or drink** | Rinse with remineralizing mouthwash, or plain water if that's what you've got | Buys your enamel recovery time. Details below under Golden Rule |
| **Every night** | Mouthwash, floss, brush with nHA, water flosser along the gumline, tongue scraper, mouth guard if you grind, then leave it | You're asleep for the next 7 to 8 hours, which makes night the best remineralization window you get all day |
| **Weekly** | Soak your tongue scraper, brush head, and water flosser tip in hydrogen peroxide for a few minutes | Tools that touch your mouth twice a day grow their own ecosystem. Evict it |
| **Monthly** | Swap your toothbrush head and check your mouth guard for wear | A frayed brush head cleans worse and a cracked guard protects nothing |
| **Every 6 months** | Dental cleaning and exam | Removes tartar you cannot remove yourself and catches problems while they're still cheap |
| **Every 6 to 18 months if you're cavity-prone, every 2 to 3 years if you're not** | Bitewing X-rays | Catches decay between teeth and under old fillings that a visual exam alone will miss. This is a risk-based range, not a flat yearly rule, so ask your dentist which end of it applies to you [^1] |
| **Every 3 to 5 years, or as needed** | Full-mouth or panoramic X-rays | Useful for wisdom teeth, implants, orthodontics, or anything that needs the bigger picture |

## The Golden Rule: Rinse First, Brush Later

Say it after coffee, wine, soda, citrus, or basically anything that isn't water: **rinse first, brush later.**

Here's the mechanism. Acid temporarily softens the mineral surface of your enamel. Brush during that window and you're not cleaning your teeth, you're sanding them. Give your saliva time to buffer the acid and rebuild the surface, then brush once it's hardened back up.

**The move:** swish with water or remineralizing mouthwash, then wait. Real waiting, 30 to 60 minutes, works best. If life doesn't allow that, wait at least 5 to 10 minutes, use nHA toothpaste (skip anything gritty like whitening paste, baking soda, or charcoal), brush light, brush short.

Sugar-free gum is a good sidekick here. It gets saliva moving, and saliva is your enamel's unpaid personal bodyguard.

## Post-Activity Cheat Sheet

Adult mouths meet adult beverages. Here's your recovery move for each.

| After this | Do this | Because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Coffee or tea** | Water rinse, wait before brushing | Stains sit on the surface, acid softens what's underneath. Handle both |
| **Red wine** | Water rinse immediately, resist the urge to scrub | Tannins stain fast and wine is acidic, a bad combination for an eager toothbrush |
| **Soda or sports drinks** | Water rinse, wait, consider skipping the sipping-all-day version | Constant small sips mean constant acid exposure, which is worse than one glass finished quickly |
| **Citrus or vinegar-based food** | Water rinse, wait the full window if you can | Among the more acid-forward things you'll put in your mouth on purpose |
| **Smoking or vaping** | Water rinse, extra attention to hydration during the day | Both dry out your mouth, and a dry mouth has less saliva around to do its buffering job |
| **Berries, curry, dark sauces** | Water rinse | Mostly a staining issue rather than an enamel one, but the rinse habit is the same either way |

Notice the pattern: water rinse, then wait, is the answer to almost everything. You just learned 90% of adult dental care in one sentence.

## Who to See, and When

Your dentist is not the only professional your mouth might need. Here's the roster.

| Provider | What they check | How often | Why them specifically |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **General dentist** | Cavities, cleanings, X-rays, overall oral health | Every 6 months, or as recommended | Your default checkup and the one who refers you onward |
| **Dental hygienist** | Cleaning, tartar removal, plaque coaching | Usually the same visit as your dentist | Does the deep clean your toothbrush cannot |
| **Periodontist** | Gum health, pocket depth, bone loss around teeth | On referral, or if you notice bleeding, recession, or loose-feeling teeth | Gum disease is a specialty of its own, and it's the leading cause of tooth loss in adults, ahead of cavities [^2] |
| **Orthodontist** | Bite alignment, crowding, jaw relationship | On referral, or if shifting, crowding, or bite discomfort shows up | Straight teeth are easier to clean, and a bad bite creates uneven wear that a general dentist will eventually just keep patching |
| **Oral surgeon** | Wisdom teeth, extractions, implants, jaw surgery | On referral, as needed | For anything past what a general dentist's toolkit is built for |

A good general dentist should notice when it's time to send you up the chain. If years pass and nobody has ever mentioned a specialist, that's worth asking about directly, not assuming means everything's fine.

## Whitening, Its Own Thing

Whitening runs on a different clock than the rest of this. Don't fold it into your daily routine.

- Carbamide peroxide 10% in a custom-fit tray, worn 2 to 10 hours (overnight works, so does a shorter daytime block), for about 14 nights
- Results tend to hold for months. Rebleach only once color noticeably fades, usually not before 6 months, rather than on a fixed weekly schedule [^3]
- Sensitivity or gum irritation is your cue to shorten the daily wear time, not push through

## The One-Line Version

Rinse first, brush later, don't skip the six-month checkup, and treat your mouth guard like it's the only thing standing between you and a very expensive Tuesday.
