# Publishing and earning from The Adult Teeth Guide

Status: proposed route, prepared 2026-10-06. This is a decision guide, not an approved launch, clinical review or revenue forecast. The approved title remains **The Adult Teeth Guide: How to Keep Your Teeth Healthy for Life**. Read the [dated platform guide](platform-guide.md) for officially sourced Amazon KDP, direct-sale and distribution rules before choosing a service.

## Audience and first offer

The [existing book purpose](../../README.md) is practical adult dental-care education: understanding teeth, maintaining a routine and evaluating recommendations. A useful first offer is a carefully reviewed reading edition with a concise printable routine reference. The value proposition is usable organization and explanation. Describe educational use accurately; keep any claim of outcomes within the existing [claims policy](../../claims-policy.md).

**Recommended first experiment:** prepare one reviewed sample chapter and an accompanying existing cheat-sheet concept for reader feedback, then choose a complete-book launch after resolving the book-wide evidence and assembly gaps. First define the questions the sample should answer: Can a reader find the main action? Can they understand its explanation? Which format would they actually use and pay for? Feedback collection and outreach require a separate authorized activity; this guide creates no audience contacts or sale.

The repository is currently public and contains the manuscript. A paid edition would therefore offer value through a finished reading/print experience, convenient distribution, a usable companion or supported updates. Selling it should not depend on claiming the text is unavailable elsewhere. Check any digital exclusivity choice against all existing public copies using the platform guide.

## Routes and format choices

| Route | What the customer receives | Why consider it | Preparation needed here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amazon KDP book | Finished e-book and/or print edition | A conventional book purchase and physical reference | Reconciled manuscript, reader-tested formatting, reviewed metadata/cover, source/rights decisions and a proof copy for print |
| Direct digital bundle | Accessible reading PDF plus a separately printable reference | Flexible packaging around the bathroom-reference use case already in README | Export tools, legible print size, delivery/support/refund process and a version/update promise |
| Wider book distribution | Reviewed book through selected additional retailers/channels | Reach readers outside one storefront | One distributor owner per format/territory, consistent metadata and compatible exclusivity decisions |
| Educational bulk/licensing offer | Approved edition or permitted companion copies for an organization | A possible institutional buyer if interest is demonstrated | Explicit usage terms, quantity/support limits and review of branding and claims |

An audio edition could be a later format if the reading experience works without seeing tables and diagrams. A recurring membership is a separate continuing content/support commitment; there is no evidence yet that this book needs one. Product sponsorship or referral income would introduce disclosure and editorial-independence questions for a book that evaluates recommendations. Treat those as later decisions, not a default source of revenue.

Before selecting a distributor, retain honest provenance for text, translations, cover and illustrations, and check the current topic, expertise, AI disclosure and low-content eligibility rules in the platform guide. A proposed route is not confirmed platform acceptance. Record actual author/contributor expertise where relevant; do not infer it from AI-assisted production or a completed export.

1. **Select the scope (C0).** Alia chooses the reader, the approved title, the single first offer and the support contact. Record whether the sample experiment comes before a complete-book sale, and set an explicit review date and effort budget.
2. **Choose route and formats (C1).** Use the platform guide to compare KDP, a direct bundle and wider distribution. Record selected formats, territories, byline/imprint, rights ownership and distribution/exclusivity decisions. Check the existing public source boundary.
3. **Finish and verify source (C2).** Review [outline](../../outline.md), [chapters](../../chapters), [assembled manuscript](../../book/full-manuscript.md) and existing [reviews](../../reviews). Resolve consequential source needs and author decisions for this candidate. The repository currently lacks an assembly script, so chapter-to-manuscript reconciliation is a real task.
4. **Build the product files (C2).** Create the chosen export, cover and companion from approved source in a deliberate production milestone. Inspect clickable contents/citations, readable figures, accessibility and the exact final page size. Store editable source, tool versions and export hashes. These files are not created by this guide.
5. **Prepare the storefront draft (C3).** Write the description, audience statement, sample, author information, format metadata and support/update terms from the approved content. Use the platform's current setup checklist to prepare tax/payment/account information privately; actual signup remains an owner action.
6. **Prove the economics and delivery (C3).** Use current platform deductions and print quotes, an owner-chosen price, refund allowance and production/support effort. Preview the final e-book, inspect a print proof if selected and test delivery/access only through an authorized account. Review the recovery checklist before approval.
7. **Record launch and learn (C4/C5).** After an actual authorized release, record the source/edition, asset hashes, product URL, receipt and MetadataDB acknowledgement. Compare real reader issues, sales and support effort against the predeclared experiment; revise or stop based on those results.

## Launch assets and upkeep

The first-launch asset set should contain a finished book export, cover source/export, selected sample, accurate description, author/byline metadata, printable companion if sold, support/correction policy and release manifest. A GitHub source archive is not automatically a retail-ready book package. Keep clinical/editorial review evidence with the book rather than implying that a platform preview checks it.

At each revision, review claims and sources that changed, update edition/correction notes, reproduce exports, and verify every active listing. Decide who receives updates and how before promising ongoing updates. Preserve old release hashes and errata. Review print cost, platform terms and support effort when changing price or format; the platform guide is a dated starting point, not a permanent fee schedule.

| Decision or evidence | Current state | Owner records before go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader and first paid offer | Proposed reviewed book/companion; not validated | Named audience, selected offer and evidence question |
| Final content and claims | Draft source and reviews exist | Candidate SHA, completed applicable reviews and author decision |
| Route and exclusivity | Undecided; source is public | Chosen channels/formats and checked compatibility |
| Commercial result | Unknown | Price, variable costs, support time, production budget and minimum acceptable outcome |
| Export and reader experience | Final toolchain unestablished | Preview/proof evidence, accessibility and final file hashes |
| Access, rights and recovery | Open gates in operating record | Account/rights locators, recovery rehearsal, responsible people |
| Signoff | **Hold for publication** | Alia's scoped go/revise/hold decision and date |

For a direct sale, calculate contribution from actual money received after channel/payment deductions, refunds, variable fulfillment and delivery/support cost. For a royalty route, begin with the platform's actual royalty estimate and avoid subtracting costs it already included. Separate fixed editing/design cost from per-sale contribution. Break-even units are fixed launch cost divided by positive contribution per sale; a contribution at or below zero requires a revised offer or economics, not a sales-volume assumption.
