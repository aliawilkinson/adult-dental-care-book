# Chapter 4: Your Daily Routine

Your daily routine should make the good choice boring. Morning: scrape the tongue, drink water, brush gently with 10% nHA toothpaste, then do not rinse, eat, or drink for 20 to 30 minutes. The quiet window lets the paste stay on enamel instead of being immediately washed away.

After meals, follow the Golden Rule: do not brush immediately after food, coffee, tea, soda, lemon water, wine, or sports drinks. Rinse with remineralizing mouthwash or plain water. Chew sugar-free gum if it helps. If you must brush because real life is happening, rinse first, wait 5 to 10 minutes, use a non-abrasive nHA toothpaste, brush lightly, and keep it short.

At night, use mouthwash, floss, brush, water floss the gumline and curves, scrape the tongue, and leave the toothpaste residue alone. Wear a mouth guard if you grind. Night is prime mineral time because you are not eating, sipping, or talking the paste away.

This is the happy path. Fluoride alternatives, nHA uncertainty, and product evidence belong in the explanatory chapters, not taped to your mirror like a legal contract.

## The High-Control Version

This routine is built for three outcomes: healthy teeth and gums, better preparation for cosmetic work, and fewer expensive surprises later. It is not the cheapest version. It is the version for someone who wants a clean, repeatable system and is willing to own more than one tool.

Morning starts with water because a dry mouth is a worse mouth. Scrape the tongue if it helps freshness, then brush with nHA toothpaste and spit without rinsing. Give the paste 20 to 30 minutes if you can. If you cannot, do not turn the day into a morality play. Just avoid immediately washing it away when you have a choice.

During the day, the rule is frequency control. Coffee in one reasonable window is less hostile than coffee sipped all morning. The same logic applies to soda, tea, sports drinks, wine, lemon water, and acidic snacks. A reusable straw can reduce front-tooth contact for some drinks, but it does not make acid harmless. At night, clean in layers: brush the broad surfaces, floss the tight contacts, use the water flosser for curves and gumline areas if it helps you, scrape the tongue for freshness, and leave the final toothpaste residue alone. If your dentist has you wearing a night guard, put it in after the routine.
